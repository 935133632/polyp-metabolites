## Main figures

**Figure 1. Metabolome-wide associations of 251 NMR metabolites with incident colon polyp and stomach/duodenal polyp.** Each point is one metabolite. The x axis shows the natural-log hazard ratio per 1 SD from Cox models adjusted for age, sex, Townsend deprivation index, body mass index, blood pressure, smoking and alcohol drinking; the y axis shows −log10 P. Orange and blue points are metabolites with higher and lower risk that are significant after Bonferroni correction (dotted line, P < 0.05/251); grey points are not significant. The five strongest metabolites in each direction are labelled.

**Figure 2. Bonferroni-significant metabolites by metabolite class.** Bars to the right of zero count metabolites associated with higher risk and bars to the left those associated with lower risk, for colon polyp (left) and stomach/duodenal polyp (right).

**Figure 3. Hazard ratios of all 251 metabolites for (A) colon polyp and (B) stomach/duodenal polyp.** Bars start at HR = 1 (inner black circle) and extend outwards for HR > 1 (red) and inwards for HR < 1 (blue); black lines are 95% confidence intervals; faded bars are not significant after Bonferroni correction; stars denote Bonferroni-adjusted P < 0.05 (*), < 0.01 (**) and < 0.001 (***). Hazard ratios are per 1 SD (scale 0.80–1.20). Metabolites are grouped as lipoprotein particles, total lipids, phospholipids, cholesterol, esterified cholesterol, free cholesterol, triglycerides, lipid composition (% of subclass lipids), sizes/apolipoproteins/other, fatty acids, and amino acids/glycolysis/ketone bodies/fluid balance. Within the lipoprotein groups, subclasses run from extremely large (XXL) to small (S) VLDL, IDL, LDL and HDL, followed by class totals.

**Figure 4. Consistency of associations between outcomes and between prevalent and incident disease.** Left, natural-log hazard ratios per SD for colon polyp against stomach/duodenal polyp; orange points are significant for both outcomes after Bonferroni correction. Right, natural-log odds ratio of prevalent colon polyp at baseline (logistic regression) against the natural-log hazard ratio of incident colon polyp.

**Figure 5. Sex and age strata, and exclusion of early events.** (A, B) Circular heat rings of natural-log hazard ratios per SD for all 251 metabolites in colon polyp (A) and stomach/duodenal polyp (B); rings from the centre outwards show the overall analysis, women, men, participants younger than 60 years and participants aged 60 years or older. Faded cells are not significant after Bonferroni correction within the ring. (C) Natural-log hazard ratios after excluding events in the first 2 years against the main analysis.

**Figure 6. Participants with both polyp types.** (A) Numbers of participants with incident colon polyp only, stomach/duodenal polyp only, and both. (B) Odds ratios per SD of all 251 metabolites comparing participants with both polyps (n = 1,110) with participants with one polyp; bars start at OR = 1 (scale 0.70–1.30), with stars as in Figure 3.

**Figure 7. Receiver operating characteristic curves for polyp prediction.** Left, out-of-fold curves from 5-fold cross-validation for the best binary model (elastic-net logistic regression); middle and right, time-dependent curves at 5 and 10 years for the penalised Cox model in the 30% test set. Feature sets: covariates only; covariates plus metabolites passing Bonferroni correction in the training data; covariates plus all 251 metabolites. Areas under the curves are given in the legends. Top row, colon polyp; bottom row, stomach/duodenal polyp.

**Figure 8. Hazard ratios of all 251 metabolites under increasing adjustment in the same participants.** Circular heat rings for (A) colon polyp and (B) stomach/duodenal polyp; rings from the centre outwards show Model 1, plus waist circumference and body fat percentage, plus HbA1c and C-reactive protein, plus prior hospital diagnoses, and all adjustments together. Faded cells are not significant after Bonferroni correction within the ring.

**Figure 9. Specificity of the metabolite profile among digestive outcomes.** Left, correlation between the 251 natural-log hazard ratios of each outcome and those of colon polyp (orange) and stomach/duodenal polyp (blue); numbers of incident events are in brackets. Right, mean natural-log hazard ratio per SD by metabolite class for each outcome, with outcomes ordered by hierarchical clustering.

**Figure 10. Metabolite risk score in the independent test set.** Top, cumulative incidence by score quartile; bottom, hazard ratios (95% CI) of quartiles 2–4 versus quartile 1. Colon polyp, left; stomach/duodenal polyp, right. Panel titles give the hazard ratio per SD and the P value for trend.

## Supplementary figures

**Figure S1. Sensitivity analysis excluding events in the first 2 years.** Circular bar plots of hazard ratios for all 251 metabolites for (A) colon polyp and (B) stomach/duodenal polyp; conventions as in Figure 3.

**Figure S2. Correlation structure of the metabolites.** (A) Circular heat rings of the correlation of each metabolite with principal components 1–8 (percentage of variance in the ring labels). (B) Cumulative variance explained by the principal components; 19 components explain 95%.

**Figure S3. Original three-character codes versus polyp-specific codes.** Natural-log hazard ratios per SD for K63 against K63.5 (left) and K31 against K31.7 (right).

**Figure S4. Internal validation.** Top, natural-log hazard ratios in the discovery (70%) and validation (30%) halves; orange points are significant in the discovery half after Bonferroni correction. Bottom, bootstrap analysis (30 resamples): metabolites ranked by the natural-log hazard ratio of the main analysis (points; orange, higher risk; blue, lower risk; grey, not significant) with the 95% bootstrap interval (band).

**Figure S5. Prevalent and incident disease.** Natural-log odds ratio of prevalent disease at baseline against the natural-log hazard ratio of incident disease for colon polyp (left) and stomach/duodenal polyp (right).

**Figure S6. Prediction performance of all models.** Area under the curve (binary models, 5-fold cross-validation) and C-index (survival models, test set) by model and feature set.

**Figure S7. Participants with both polyp types.** Left, numbers in the polyp groups; right, order of diagnosis in participants with both polyps.

**Figure S8. Dose–response curves.** Restricted cubic splines (four knots) of selected metabolites for colon polyp and stomach/duodenal polyp; hazard ratio relative to the median (line) with 95% confidence interval (band); the P value for non-linearity is given in each panel title.

**Figure S9. Audit of the lipoprotein results.** Top left and middle, hazard ratios per SD for NMR measures and clinical-chemistry assays of the same blood sample; top right, crude incidence of stomach/duodenal polyp by sex-specific tertile of HDL cholesterol; bottom, hazard ratios of HDL cholesterol, LDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B, and total triglycerides and glycoprotein acetyls, with stepwise adjustment for prior-diagnosis proxies.
